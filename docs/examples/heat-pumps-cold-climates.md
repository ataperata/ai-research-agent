# How well do heat pumps work in cold climates?

_Mode: offline demo · 5 sources cited · 2 excluded · verification: 17/17 sentences supported_

## Key findings

- Older air-source heat pumps often struggled below about 25°F (-4°C), which is one reason many people still believe heat pumps do not work in cold climates. [1]
- The Nordic experience shows that heat pumps can heat homes through long, cold winters when buildings are well insulated and systems are correctly sized and installed. [2]
- To qualify under the specification, a cold-climate heat pump must have a coefficient of performance of at least 1.75 at 5°F (-15°C) when running at maximum capacity. [3]
- Because a heat pump moves heat rather than generating it, it can deliver two to four units of heat for every unit of electricity it uses. This ratio is called the coefficient of performance, or COP, and electric resistance heaters have a COP of 1. [4]

## Overview

Field studies of cold-climate units in northern U.S. states have generally found seasonal average COPs above 2, with COPs between roughly 1.5 and 2 on the coldest days near 0°F (-18°C). [1] Modern cold-climate models use variable-speed, or inverter-driven, compressors and improved refrigerant circuits. Many are rated to keep heating at outdoor temperatures of -13°F (-25°C) or lower. [1] In Norway, about 60 percent of buildings are equipped with a heat pump, and Sweden and Finland also have high adoption rates. [2] Heat pump sales in Europe grew by nearly 40 percent in 2022, driven by high natural gas prices and government incentives. [2]

## How it works

There is less heat in colder air and the compressor has to work harder to raise it to indoor temperature, so both heating capacity and COP decline. [1] In winter, an air-source heat pump pulls heat from the outdoor air and moves it indoors, and in summer it reverses and works as an air conditioner. [4]

## Performance and data

Heat pump efficiency falls as outdoor temperatures drop. [1] The cold climate specification sets minimum performance levels for air-source heat pumps sold in northern regions. [3] Installers say the most common cause of poor cold-weather performance is a system that was undersized or installed without accounting for the home's insulation and air leaks. [5]

## Practical considerations

A unit sized only for mild weather may need backup heat during the coldest hours of the year. [1] Program staff recommend sizing a cold-climate heat pump to meet most or all of a home's heating load at the local design temperature, and keeping backup heat only for extreme conditions. [3] Many heat pump installations in cold regions keep a backup heat source, such as electric resistance strips or an existing gas furnace. [5] Ground-source, or geothermal, heat pumps exchange heat with the ground, which stays at a fairly constant temperature year round. That makes them more efficient than air-source units in very cold weather, but they cost more to install. [4]

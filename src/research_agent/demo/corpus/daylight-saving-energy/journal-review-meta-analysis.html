Does Daylight Saving Save Electricity? What 44 Studies Say

By Review Editorial Staff

2018-09-20

A meta-analysis published in The Energy Journal in 2018 combined the results of 44 studies on daylight saving time and electricity use. The authors, Tomas Havranek, Dominik Herman and Zuzana Irsova, collected 162 estimates from those studies.

Across the 44 studies, the average effect of daylight saving time was a reduction in electricity consumption of about 0.34 percent while it was in force.

The size of the effect depends on geography: countries farther from the equator tended to save more electricity, while subtropical regions often used more electricity under daylight saving time.

Studies of the 2000 Sydney Olympics, when two Australian states extended daylight saving time, found no drop in overall electricity consumption. Higher demand in the morning offset lower demand in the evening.

The reviewers note that newer studies tend to find smaller savings than older ones, which fits the shift away from lighting as a major source of electricity demand.

References

  1. Havranek, T., Herman, D. and Irsova, Z. (2018). Does Daylight Saving Save Electricity? A Meta-Analysis. The Energy Journal, 39(2).
  2. Kellogg, R. and Wolff, H. (2008). Daylight time and energy: Evidence from an Australian experiment. Journal of Environmental Economics and Management, 56(3).

Heat Pump Systems: How They Work

By Building Technologies Office

2023-06-01

A heat pump moves heat from one place to another instead of producing heat by burning fuel. In winter, an air-source heat pump pulls heat from the outdoor air and moves it indoors, and in summer it reverses and works as an air conditioner.

Even cold air contains heat energy. A refrigerant absorbs that heat in the outdoor coil, a compressor raises its temperature, and the heat is released inside the home.

Because a heat pump moves heat rather than generating it, it can deliver two to four units of heat for every unit of electricity it uses. This ratio is called the coefficient of performance, or COP, and electric resistance heaters have a COP of 1.

Heat pumps can reduce electricity use for heating by roughly 50 percent compared with electric resistance heating such as furnaces and baseboard heaters.

Ground-source, or geothermal, heat pumps exchange heat with the ground, which stays at a fairly constant temperature year round. That makes them more efficient than air-source units in very cold weather, but they cost more to install.

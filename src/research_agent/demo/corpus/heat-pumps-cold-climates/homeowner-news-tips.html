Heat Pumps in Winter: What Owners Should Expect

By Dana Whitfield

2024-11-05

Many heat pump installations in cold regions keep a backup heat source, such as electric resistance strips or an existing gas furnace. In a dual-fuel setup, the system switches to the furnace when it is very cold outside or when gas is cheaper to run.

Owners sometimes notice that air from a heat pump feels cooler than air from a furnace. Heat pumps deliver air at lower temperatures, typically around 90 to 100°F, but run for longer periods to keep the home warm.

Steam rising from the outdoor unit in winter is usually a normal sign of a defrost cycle, not a malfunction.

Installers say the most common cause of poor cold-weather performance is a system that was undersized or installed without accounting for the home's insulation and air leaks.
